# Scan → CAD reverse engineering (ScanRuler)

**Read the model guide first** — `guide_read { topic: "model" }`: how ScanRuler works for a modeller, building, the kernel's habits and the handover checks are there. Asked for together, `topic: ["model", "scan-to-cad"]` answers the model guide and names this one in `next`: read it next. This guide is what the scan adds: reading it, deciding what the part is, measuring it for the model, and judging the model against it.

Guiding principle: **measure from reliable scan evidence, construct the way the part's designer did, and make uncertainty visible.**

**ScanRuler measures, you decide.** Every number about the scan — a line, an arc, a blend's radius, a draft, a wall's lean, a mirror plane, a distance from model to scan — comes from a ScanRuler call, with its evidence (residual, sigma, the nominal and how many sigmas off). Your work is the decisions: what the part is, which relations the designer meant, which nominal, what to build next. The scan stays in ScanRuler; you never copy it out to compute on it. The few things no command does yet are under *Fallbacks* at the end.

Quality bar (from user review): clean, designer-like topology matters more than the last 1–3 % of fit — symmetric where the part is; analytic faces (planes, drafted extrusions, cylinders, revolves, sweeps) where it has them, a smooth loft or a fitted surface where it has not, named as such, never a faceted loft; no ledges or steps where surfaces meet; the visible radii present; nominal numbers where the evidence allows (Ø6.6 clearance, 2° draft, R3), measured ones where it does not.

Deliverables next to the scan: `<part>_CAD.step`, `<part>_CAD.scanruler` (`project_save`), `<part>_REPORT.md`, deviation images of both maps — on the scan (iso, bottom, top, side) and on the model (iso and the side most inferred) — written with `path` and `inline: false` as the saved copies; the maps themselves you look at inline, in every loop (§5) and once more before the handover. Never modify the original scan.

**The deviation map and the volume error drive the design, from the first feature to the last.** A run is loops: build a step, `reverse_body_review` (the volume error and the map in one call, its worst places numbered on a picture you look at), work on the top place, review again — until the plan's target is met or the person signs off on less. They are not a report written at the end. The last mirror holder was built to a closed STEP, measured once, and handed over at 62 % within T with its maps unseen.

**model-reference** has every command's shape (Command shapes), how u and v lie on each plane (Plane frames), what answers cost (Reply cost) and what refusals mean (Errors) — a section at a time: `guide_read { topic: "model-reference", section: "Scan and measuring" }`.

## 0. Setup

- The model guide's §1 (tools, the session, notes, batches, jobs, helpers, context), then `scan_open` with the full path — `saveTo` when the page holds work. The first read of this guide gives the setup, the inspection, the datums, the archetypes, the design loop and the handover; its §3 Measuring comes under `later` with when to read it, and is read by section when that moment comes.
- **Task list:** inspect, classify, decide T → **the plan**: archetype, the region inventory and the target, set with `reverse_plan_set` and posted in the chat with the defaults (§1) → datums → **the design loop** (§5): a step, `reverse_body_review`, the top place fixed, until the target → the handover checks, export, document.
- **The gates, before the first feature:** (1) the freeform triage's verdict (§1) — smooth or faceted, which route — (2) the region inventory, small items too (tabs, notches, lips, seats), (3) the target: `reverse_plan_set { archetype, regions, target: { withinT, tolerance: T } }` — `archetype` one of plate, turned, machined, sheetMetal, shelledHousing, facetedHousing, rotationallyPatterned, castWithMachinedFeatures, smoothSolidCasting, mouldedHousingWithKeys, volute, freeformThinShell, freeformSurface, other (§4a); `withinT` defaults by class to 90 % of the scan within T on analytic parts, 85 % on castings and mouldings, 75 % on freeform shells and the surface route; the person may set another in the first ask; `tolerance` keeps a reading at a looser T from counting. All three in the chat. A region found later: `reverse_plan_set` again — ticks stay. A feature added with no plan answers a warning: that warning means the gates were skipped.
- **Ticking off:** `reverse_plan_tick { region, feature }` when a region is built, `{ region, skip: reason }` when it is left out — every skip is in the final reply. A feature removed after failing and made needless by another: `reverse_plan_replace { removed, by, reason }`, `by` a feature that is built — the verdict lists it with the reason, and the final reply names it.
- **The run ends when the verdict says met** — the target reached, every region ticked or skipped, nothing removed after failing that was not built again, and no `kind: "feature"` patch over T left on a plan region — or with the person's sign-off on less after you said what is left. The share within T alone is not the verdict: the last fan reached 88.9 % in twenty minutes with its blade roots removed and its blades 0.5 mm off, and handed over. Below the verdict the reply says **"not done"** with the open places, never "done with limitations". **No budget of your own:** when a run's cost worries you, say so in the chat and ask — the last run switched to "wrap up" at about 125 calls and told no one.
- **A feature that fails and is removed is said in the chat at once,** with what its absence leaves (the review's place there). Removing one answers a reminder; the final compare lists every one.

## 1. Inspect, classify, decide

- **Scan check first: `scan_qa`** — open rims, σ_scan, and how thickness runs through the part: `tiltDeg` over about 0.3° between faces a machined or moulded part has parallel is the scan (or its alignment), not the part — say so before modelling, and ask for another scan if there is one. The last plate's scan was 1.3° out, and a session went into whether its faces tapered. `mesh_inspect` where it is installed adds islands. Read rims from the answers, not from the back-face tint: a rim with a `circle` is a hole the scanner did not see into — `element_fit { kind: "circle", rim }` makes it an element. A `sheet` in the answer is the gauge (`sheet.gauge.p50`), never `thickness`, which then reads the box across the part (the last cage's 52.5 mm against its 0.3 mm sheet; an earlier pass guessed 0.7). Its `apart` regions — tabs bent in, ribs standing inside — go on the region list now; the last cage's four tabs surfaced only in the final compare.
- **Look** from iso, top, bottom, front and both sides — one `view_render { views: [...] }` sheet — a step at the end of an arm reads as a flange only from the side.
- **Region inventory, before any build.** Shaded views of the scan alone, one per side, closer where there is detail; write the list of regions with their function — each key and its ends, icons and lettering, button tiers, facets, grooves and parting lines, the screen and its bezel, covers and seams, slots and channels, tabs, notches and lips, the corner rounds and the large blends. It goes into `reverse_plan_set { regions }` (each with `at`, a point on it, so the review names the region a place lies in) and into the chat; tick each off when built. The last mirror holder's three tabs, a notch in its bore and a rim lip were never on a list, so never built. After every major step, the deviation map on the same views. The last housing's first pass found none of its regions ("still terrible"); shaded views and local maps found them in minutes. Planar regions: `scan_segment` (§3). A region's own relief: `scan_heightmap { plane, of: "relief" }`.
- **Is it square already?** Check the scan against its own faces (a plane fit on the largest face, `align_datum` only if it is off) before aligning; then align to the faces the part mates on.
- **Repeats round an axis** (blades, spokes, holes, pads): `scan_pattern { axis, at, radius }` counts them — `count`, `pitchDeg`, `phaseDeg`, each copy's `rms` — one band of radius per feature, since in one cut the feature with the most outline sets the count.
- **Noise floor:** `scan_qa`'s noise, or `element_fit` a plane on the largest clean face (its form error); fit the best bore too. Nothing is trustworthy finer than about 3 σ_scan.
- **An STL from CAD** (`scan_qa`'s `tessellation`, noise 0): exact numbers. `sparse`: `mesh_densify { maxEdge: densifyTo }` first, else fits find no points inside a face. T from the process; radii off rim vertices, on CAD's circle (a facet lies a sagitta inside), snapped to nominal.
- **Draft, early, on anything moulded or cast:** `scan_draft { pull }` along each direction a tool opens (the main pull, each side core). Walls leaning 0.5–3° explain rails, rims and undersides that otherwise miss their fits by the lean; build that lean in from the start. A thin wall's two sides come back as two patches facing apart; `round: true` is a boss's or hole's round side. **A cylinder fit on a cast wall says when it is no cylinder:** `region.taper` gives the draft along the part's axis, read off the normals, and a warning; `double: true` with `parting` is a wall drafted both ways from a parting line, and each part's `box` goes in a `kind: "cone"` fit as `within`. Fitted on part of a turn, a cylinder leans to take a taper up and reads a small σ, so a small σ does not clear it: read the axis against the datum. Build such a wall as two drafted extrudes from one sketch on the parting plane. Drafting one triple clamp's walls took it from 74 % within T to 79 %, 81 % with its rounds.
- **Classify** in two lines: *process* (tool marks, sharp inside corners → machined; draft, parting line, even walls → moulded or die cast; rough skin, big fillets → cast or forged; layer lines → printed; constant thickness with bends → sheet metal; often two at once) and *archetype* (§4a). Confirm the process with the user if unsure.
- **How are such parts designed?** Name the part's class (a water pump's housing, a gearbox cover, a bicycle crank) and write down, before the plan, how parts of that class are designed — from what you know, or from patents and handbooks: what the form grows from, which features share lines and planes, what the function sets. The last pump's housing was built twice from the scan's shapes and withdrawn twice; the third, from a patent search (a volute growing from a cutwater, a tangential outlet, a stepped bearing seat, ribs and bosses on shared radial lines), stood.
- **Tolerance T** — what "over tolerance" means from here on; say it in the report:

  | Process | T (± mm) | Colour range |
  |---|---|---|
  | Machined, ground | 0.02–0.05 | 0.15 |
  | Injection moulded, die cast | 0.05–0.1 | 0.3 |
  | Sand cast, forged, FDM printed | 0.3–0.5 | 1.0 |
  | Worn, hand-finished, deformed | wider — say so | |

  T = max(process value, 3 σ_scan), plus about 0.1 % of the span on castings and mouldings; machined faces of a casting at the machined T. Everything follows from it: compare `range: T`, deviation `tolerance: T, range: 3T`, `maxDistance` ≈ 10 T but under the wall, the small-angle rule (§2). Never inherit the 0.1 mm defaults.
- **Feature inventory with intent:** every hole, bore, pocket, slot, boss, rib, flange and web, seen from two directions, each with its *function* and the *nominal* that implies — clearance holes (M4 → 4.5, M5 → 5.5, M6 → 6.6, M8 → 9), tap drills (M6 → 5.0), catalogue bores (6, 8, 10, 12, 15, 17, 20, 22, 25, 30…), bosses 2–2.5 × the screw, ribs 0.5–0.7 of the wall, draft 0.5/1/1.5/2/3°, round radii, equal bolt circles, 15/30/45/60° angles; datums are the faces that mate. **Snap to the nominal when the measurement is within T, record both, never snap across T** — positions too: a symbol centred in its disc, a row of windows on its pitch. Tick features off as built; the last part's missing hole was never on a list.
- **Ask before the first build — in the chat, one round.** Before the first feature, post the defaults below and the modelling approach (next point) in the chat, where the person reads — the notes on the last parts were nearly all about choices made without asking, and a viewport note scrolls away unseen. One `reverse_note_add` on the part may point to it in one line. Build on them unless the answer differs:
  - **One continuous body**, even where the real part is two mouldings (lid and base): no parting-line step, rounds unbroken across it. A wrapped assembly (shells, a cover, screws) too: one solid of its outside, no inferred interior — say so in the defaults; separate shells only when asked. Two runs on one pak read alike either way (86–87 % within T), and the shells' interior was all inferred.
  - **Steps and warps under T are one surface** — a 0.14 mm raised deck is warp, not a feature.
  - **One round all the way round** each end, edge loop or rim, at one nominal radius.
  - **Functional markings modelled** — polarity signs, contact marks, arrows, scales that mean something; lettering, logos and label outlines left off.
  - **Features on their design positions**: centred on the disc, channel or datum they belong to and on their pitch, not where the scan put them.
  - Anything the scan cannot settle (through or blind, which datum) goes in the same note.
- **How was it designed?** One paragraph — sketch plane, first feature, order (moulding: outline on the parting plane, draft, shell, bosses, ribs; machined: stock, every feature a cut; turned: one profile revolved, then off-axis features; sheet metal: one skin shelled to gauge). Build in that order; it is the report's "modelling approach".
- **Covers and housings come with their interior** — ribs, bosses, webs, ledges, seats, skirts and clips are modelled with the outer skin, without asking (the last cover's was asked for half-way, after its skin). Plan them from the start, and report outer-skin and interior numbers apart. **A region whose σ is much tighter than the rest** (a bulb socket in a lamp housing) is likely an inserted part: leave it out or model its interface, and say so.
- **A bowl with its inside scanned** (cavity walls, ribs, bores in the scan): never shell the outer form, which reaches none of it (the last airbox: 8.5 % within T, 44 % of its scan inside). Fit the inner skin as a body and `split { tools: [outer, inner], keep: "between" }`, or cut the inner form; ribs `intersect` it, then `pattern`. `align_auto`'s `largestInside`: a rib wall — level on the flange.
- **Smooth or faceted — decide before any plane cut.** `scan_segment` answers `curvedRuns`: regions whose σ sits near `maxRms`, joined smoothly at a few degrees, are strips of one curved surface, not facets — the run names the surface — an extruded profile `along` a direction (a section square to it, the profile fitted and extruded), a `cylinder` (`scan_segment { mark: run.regions }`, then `element_fit_marked { kind: "cylinder", marked: true }`), or freeform (sections across it, then sweep, loft or a Patch network). A region marked `curved` is a strip, its neighbour angle the fit's band; only neighbours with `smooth: false` meet at a crease. Neighbours under 30° apart are one surface until a section across them shows a crease. The last mirror holder's walls came out of `scan_segment` as ten "planes" at σ 0.036–0.042 (nine times the noise) meeting at 7–9°; built as cut planes, its whole skin read faceted. The verdict goes in the chat and in the plan's `archetype`.
- **Freeform triage — be blunt.** **Doubly curved walls are tried as a sphere or torus first** (`element_fit`): the last lamp's back wall was an exact R103 sphere. For every non-analytic surface choose early: (1) `reverse_section_stack` in two directions — constant or linear tracks → drafted extrusion, revolve or sweep; (2) the section's shape changes along one direction only → a loft (model §2b); (3) curvature changes both ways → the surface route (§4a): a Patch network first — patches sharing their curves, tangent across them, creases where the part folds — and Fit Surface for a single gentle patch. A thin wall of such skins (a cover, a fairing) is the freeform thin shell (§4a): its thickness route is chosen before building. Tell the user before building what comes out to T, what as patches with their σ, and what cannot be done. For a grip, blade, fairing or organic print say "this is a complex free-surface part that ScanRuler's parametric tools cannot properly reverse engineer today; the surface route can give a fitted surface model — a skin of patches on the scan — not a designer's parametric model".
- **Missing data, said once:** list at the start what the scan does not show — inside pockets, bosses, deep channels — model it to the evidence at both ends and the part's function, and move on; the model side's **no scan** area (§5) is where it shows in the report.
- **Broken parts — read the break before modelling it.** A fracture is rough or jagged where its neighbours are smooth, has no draft or rounds where they have them, stops a wall mid-span, or leaves a feature with no twin across a symmetry the rest of the part keeps. The last bracket's broken-away end was modelled as a tooth and a curl, and undone. Rebuild the whole missing piece from its neighbours (model §2, item 13: the same wall, round and lip), mark it inferred (I) in the report, and ask in the chat when a "feature" sits at a break. A chunk gone shows as a cluster in the model side's `noScan`.
- **Also:** lettering and wear (not modelled); artefacts (blobs and domed caps in holes, stitching, spikes); an open rim is missing data, not a hole — through/blind is inferred unless both ends are seen; threads on every small hole (§6); symmetry planned from the start, scan asymmetry never leaking into the model.
- **A wrapped skin** (one closed skin of an assembly, no rims: `scan_qa` says "closed skin"): the wrap bridged every hole it did not see through. Read each hole from both ends — `scan_heightmap` from +axis and from −axis — and read a cylinder fit's `closed: { from, to }`: two caps facing apart, closer together than the bore's Ø, are the wrap's bridge, not the part's floor. A bore that steps wider partway down fits on its seed's part and answers the other as `second` (radius, where along the axis); a fit refused "two surfaces?" lists what it found — give `within` over the part you want. `scan_qa` says closed too when the only rims are pinholes against the part's size. **Name what the wrap hid in the plan from the start:** a bridge inside an opening as a skipped region with why, and a bore you model that the scan did not see as a region with `inferred: true` and a box round all of it (widen it with `reverse_plan_update`). Its missing volume is then reported by name under the review's `inferred`, is not ranked as a place and does not block the verdict; say those regions in the chat. The skin still counts against the share within T.

## 2. Datums

- `align_auto` for a coarse pose, then `align_datum` from *function*: the face that mates, not the largest. Plate: mounting plane, a line through two hole axes, a hole as origin. Turned: the longest clean cylinder as Z, a shoulder as origin. Cast or moulded: the parting plane or machined datum face. Rotationally patterned: the bore's axis, square to the face it mates on.
- **Datum check before levelling on a flat face:** a radial section or `scan_heightmap { levels: true }` over the region confirms it is the largest coplanar face, not a recess or a pad floor — `align_datum` answers `parallelFace` when a parallel face 0.3–5 mm off holds more of the scan (the last fan wheel was levelled on a lettering pad's floor 1.41 mm off the flange; a stamped sheet on its bead crowns 0.4 mm proud — level a sheet on the largest level's `plane`, `align_datum { primary: { plane } }`). A plane fit that warns "the crown of a bead or round, not a face?" (normals spread over 10°, or under 15 % of its box filled) grew on a bead — the last pump's first datum did, on the O-ring's crown: seed on the face itself. A first alignment centres the coordinates no datum sets on the scan's box (`centred: ["y"]`); `keepFree: true` leaves them.
- **Symmetric parts:** `element_construct` with `plane-symmetry` finds the mirror plane without moving the part; report its `symmetry.rms` as the part's asymmetry; then `align_datum` to it or `align_symmetry`.
- **The origin sets only what the datums leave open** — with a plane and a line, only the coordinate along the line; the answer's `origin.set` says which. To put the zero on a point in all three, align, then `align_datum { origin }` alone.
- **Cross-check** against back faces, other planes and bore axes; note the residuals.
- **Small angles:** a tilt that moves the far end by less than T is noise — round it; more than 2–3 T is real — investigate. 0.5° is nothing on 20 mm and 2.6 mm on 300 mm.
- **Cut planes on a symmetric part are exactly symmetric** (`plane-coords`, off-axis component 0), or mirrored halves leave a seam.

## 3. Measuring

<!-- read when: before the first measurement for a feature — a fit, a section read, a height map -->

- `element_fit` seeded in the feature's middle, `sigma: 2` on noisy or threaded walls. **Say which surface you mean**: `side: "inside"` for a bore or cup, `"outside"` for a boss or ball, `radius: { min, max }` for the size, `axis` for a hole seeded at its centre, `within` for a box — near a rim a bare seed takes whichever surface fits (the last bowl's cup came out R10, its underside, for R6.5). Check the radius against a section before writing it anywhere. A refusal "(axis off by N°: axis … through …)" names the axis it found, a point on it and its points: give that `axis`, or drop `axis`. **Several seeds** (`at: [...]`) grow one surface: each seed answers `inRegion`, `offMm` and `deg`, and one on another surface warns — fit each alone, or `scan_segment { mark: [a, b] }` and `element_fit_marked { marked: true }`. `scan_nearest` for points. A face: `element_fit_marked { kind, box, normal }` on the whole face, not a patch — a sloped face is a plane fitted so, its normal the slope; `scan_query { …, mark: true, fields: "count" }` first to see what the box holds without a vertex list in the context.
- **Sections, never temporary sketches:** `section_cut` + `section_get`; `reverse_section_fit` for lines and arcs (a line's `leanDeg` is draft); `reverse_section_stack` for many heights at once — read its `tracks` (tilt, draft per side, taper, the radius of a round). **Through an axis:** `section_cut { across: { axis, angleDeg } }` is a half section of a turned or patterned part, u out along the radius and v up the axis at every angle; a sketch takes the same plane as `{ kind: "radial", axis, angleDeg }`. **One skin of a thin wall:** `skin: "outer" | "inner"` (or `facing { dir: [u, v], withinDeg }`) on `section_get`, `reverse_section_fit`, `reverse_section_outline`, `reverse_section_stack` and a sketch's `fit` keeps the cut to that side, each run a chain of its own — ribs, bosses and the inner skin left out.
- **To understand a shape, look at it cut: `section_render`** (about 1.5k tokens, mm grid, the model's outline and red over T) was the cheapest reliable reading on the last parts — far cheaper than a stack or fit with every chain. A side view the way CAD draws it: `up: "z"`. **Several heights at once:** `section_render { plane, offsets: [...] }`, up to nine cuts side by side on one grid — a corner's notches, posts and recesses in one picture instead of ten calls. **Spacing under the smallest feature:** offsets closer than the smallest detail expected — snap tabs and clips are 2–5 mm; the last bracket's slices 10 mm apart missed four 9 mm tabs the person then had to point out.
- **Places in world coordinates, never worked-out [u, v].** Every sheet has its own origin: a section's is the part centre's foot, an element's its centre, a radial section's the axis, a sketch's the world origin's foot. Give `section_render { frame: { at: [x, y, z], size } }` or `frame: { world: { min, max } }`, `box: { world: { min, max } }` to `scan_heightmap` and `scan_outline`, `chain: { at: [x, y, z] }` to `reverse_section_outline`, and `within: { world }` to fits and stacks. Every answer carries its sheet; `plane.center` gives a plane element's centre in [u, v]; a height map that meets little of the scan says where it lies (`scanLies`).
- **Coordinates come from answers, never from pixels.** **Outlines are lines, arcs and splines — never polylines**, by the person's request: a cut-out, an opening, a rib's or a plan's profile is a sketch `fit` on its chain (`chains: { at }`, `within` for the run, `accept` tangent), closed by lines collinear with the end pieces — the profile road below — or `reverse_section_fit { chains, within }` read first. `reverse_section_outline`'s polygon only for a throw-away cutter nobody sees. Reading corners off a picture was the last bowl's main source of errors.
- **A scan full of small holes** cuts into open chains and noise: give `closeGaps` (0.3–1 mm, under the smallest real gap), `minLength` and `closedOnly` on stacks and fits, `closeGaps` and `within` on a sketch's `fit`. A `fit` with `chains: "all"` names and skips chains too short to fit. A fitted chain left open answers `open: { ends, gap, tryCloseGaps }`: fit again with `closeGaps` at about `tryCloseGaps` — it bridges with a straight run, so on a curve close it after with a line or arc held tangent to the end pieces.
- **Heights the sections hide** — pocket floors, cell ceilings, ledges, the underside: `scan_heightmap { box, axis }` (`+z` looks up from below) — one grid of numbers, `picture: true` to see it. Where it answers null, the scanner never saw: say so (inferred), don't guess from a slice. **On a face that is not square to an axis** — a button on a corner face, a slope, a key's side — `scan_heightmap { plane }` maps in that plane's own frame. `of: "relief"` brings out icons and grooves, and `of: "deviation"` shows how the model sits there. `levels: true` gives the tiers' heights and areas instead of the grid — cells steeper than `slopeDeg` (10°) are left out first and counted as `sloped`, so a fillet's ramp no longer joins two tiers. These maps gave the last housing's outlines, tiers, offsets and groove depths in numbers. **Recesses and ledges: a cut along them as well.** Tiers leave the ramps between them out (`sloped`), so a recess read from below alone comes out a box; one `section_render` lengthwise along it gives its profile — flats, ramps, their radii. `levels` answers `ramps` (each with its box and the levels it joins) when the tiers leave much out: cut along those boxes. The last housing's rail recesses were drawn as boxes until the person asked for that cut.
- **Faces that look flat are tried flat first.** Planar regions over the scan: `scan_segment { kind: "plane", box?, symmetric? }` — each with its plane, σ, area and neighbours, mirror twins paired. Small facets rank low on a whole part, so give a `box` round one band or raise `limit`. A region becomes an element by `mark: id` (several at once: `mark: [a, b]`) and `element_fit_marked { marked: true }`; a mark answers the marked region alone, and `list: true` gives the list again. One region: `element_fit { kind: "plane", at }` grows from its seed and answers the region it covers. A `warning` that it spans two surfaces means a smaller `within` or another seed. Before a fitted freeform on a face that reads flat to the eye, seed-grow a plane: the last housing's flank came out one plane at rms 0.024, where four planes and a B-spline had stood and read wrong to the person. **The other way round:** a "plane" whose σ is near `maxRms` — five times the noise or more — is a strip of a curved face; read its `curvedRuns` entry before cutting with it.

**The profile road — a slice to a constrained sketch, all in ScanRuler:**

0. **A part of several heights: list the levels first** — `scan_heightmap { levels: true }` over the part — and choose the sketch's level: the one with the most full-height walls, never inside a top round. The other levels go into the same sketch afterwards (`fit: { level, share: T, within }`, model §2c), never into sketches of their own.
1. `section_cut` where the profile is clean (or a sketch straight on a coordinate plane), then `reverse_section_fit { section }` to read the pieces first: each line and arc with `residual`, `sigma` and `nominal`, and `candidates` — the relations the pieces nearly hold, each with `off`, `sigma` and `sigmas`. Raise `detail` (0.7–0.9) when a small round or step was merged into its neighbour; lower it when a wall broke into pieces. A long curve (an R400 underside, a housing side) stays one arc; two tangent arcs of near radius come out as one — fit that chain with `arcs: 2` (and `within` for the curve alone), read both residuals, take tangent from the candidates. Read coordinates from the answers' `world` values, not off pictures. **A section's sheet counts from the part's centre; a sketch counts from the origin** (model-reference, Plane frames) — a sheet number typed into a sketch lands off by the centre (the last part's channels sat 1.29 mm off). Convert with the answer's `sketch.add`, or take `world`.
2. `reverse_feature_add` a sketch on `{ kind: "section", id }` with `sketch: { fit: { accept: { sigmas: 2, kinds: ["tangent", "horizontal", "vertical"] } } }`: the same pieces become the sketch, each still carrying its slice points, and every relation of those kinds within 2 sigmas of holding is taken as the design's and solved — the answer's `accept` lists what was `accepted` (with its constraint ids) and what was `left` (why: sigmas, kind, conflict, implied, or `moved` — it would drag a piece past `maxMoveMm`: 3 sigmas of the pieces the relation touches, their own residual on their points (never under the chain's scatter), unless given; `moves.limitMm` says the one used). A move is how far a piece leaves where its curve was — a join sliding along the pieces is none. Where a whole kind is left `moved`, `accept.note` says how far they went and the `maxMoveMm` that takes them: compare that with T before giving it. Read `moved` (always there): a piece moved more than T is a relation the part does not have. Start with those three kinds; add equal, parallel and the rest by hand from the candidates. Slice to constrained profile is this one call.
3. **Decide the relations.** Read `accepted`: take out what the design does not mean (`reverse_sketch_edit { remove: { constraints } }` with its ids). For the rest, `reverse_sketch_candidates { sketch }` lists what the sketch does not hold yet. Accept what the design means — under about 2 sigmas is within the scan's noise; a relation 6 sigmas off was measured apart, so it is a decision, not a reading — by `reverse_sketch_edit { add: { constraints } }`, copying each candidate's `constraints`. Tangent arcs, equal radii, symmetric pairs, a wall on a datum: all relations, never a computed arc.
4. **Corners the scan shows round:** `reverse_sketch_edit { add: { rounds: [{ a, b }] } }` — one call per sketch, any number of corners, lines or arcs either side: an arc tangent to both, fitted to the slice points about the corner (taken off the pieces that carried them when the fit merged the round in), with `radius`, `sigma`, `nominal`, `sigmas`, `points` and `held` (`points`, `given` or `dimension`) in the answer. `radius` given holds it instead. A round reads off its pieces' slice points: **keep a corner's pieces until its round is fitted** — a `warning` "radius not read" (under three points, or a radius at the search's limit) means it had nothing to fit; give `radius` or fit the corner again. Two lines meeting at a kink are not tangent (the refusal says so): `add.rounds` blends the kink with the scan's round. Never compute a blend.
5. **Read what the relations cost:** the edit's answer gives `solve.fit` (how the fitted pieces sit on their slice points with the relations held exactly) and `moved` (which pieces moved, how far). The solver refits the pieces to their points under the relations — that is the blend fitted tangent, by ScanRuler. A piece that moved more than T, or a fit that got worse by more than T, is a relation the part does not have: remove it (`remove: { constraints }`).
6. **Dimensions last**, snapped to nominals within T (`nominal` on each piece says how far and in how many sigmas), as `length`/`radius`/`angle` relations or parameters.

- **Slice every pocket and hole at several heights** (one `reverse_section_stack`) — a circle in one slice may be a turned rounded rectangle. **Fit every wall's angle**; several faces at one odd angle mean a feature drawn in its own frame. **Check whether two faces are one surface** before fitting them apart: one `element_fit_marked` over both — a form error near the noise floor says one.
- **Pocket and rib draft from a section across the wall,** never from a stack's bounding boxes: they take in the rounds. A die casting's pockets read 3.5° that way; across the walls they were 2.0–4.4° and the ribs nearly vertical.
- **Ruled walls before lofts:** the stack's draft per side and per height. Constant lean with a curved plan → one drafted extrusion. Lean changing along the part → two drafted zones; over T, back to the triage.
- **Fit plan chains tangent** where the scan is smooth (step 3's tangent relations); chains joined by intersection leave kinks no fillet chain survives.

## 4. Building from the scan

Build as the model guide's §2 says; what the scan adds:

- **Numbers the scan gives, as "fit":** a fillet's `radius: "fit"`, an even chamfer's `distance: "fit"`, a draft's `angleDeg: "fit"`, an extrude's `extent: { kind: "toScan", from, near? }` — ScanRuler reads each edge or face and answers `fitted` with value, sigma, nominal and sigmas; edges or faces that read apart are refused with each reading (split them). The feature stores the number; take the nominal by setting it when `sigmas` is under about 2. After a change upstream, `reverse_feature_refit { feature }` fits again. Never edit a number back and forth by hand to chase the scan.

### 4a. By archetype
- **Plate:** outline extruded by the thickness — a plate of several heights from one sketch, every level fitted into it (`level`), each region extruded to its height; a pad on a face from a sketch on that face, its outline projected (model §2c); holes from parameters; raised regions as `new` bodies trimmed and joined; drafted blocks sketched on top and extruded down; pockets cut from below; the outline intersect; holes; rounds. `draftDeg` leans every side, positive inward as the solid leaves the sketch (its expression `exprs.draftDeg`, the same sign) — to lean only some, extrude straight and add `draft`. Check the lean from the answer's bounds once. Trim neighbours with the same drafted plane, or slivers kill the fillets.
- **Turned:** Z the axis; one profile through the axis with every shoulder, groove and chamfer, revolved once; rounds as sketch arcs; off-axis features cut in their own frame; nominal diameters.
- **Sheet metal, constant shells:** thickness measured once and snapped to a gauge; one skin with bends as tangent arcs, then `shell`; inside radius ≈ thickness; warp reported, not modelled. Holes and slots punched in a thin sheet: a sketch on it with `fit: { through: true }` — the sheet seen through, scan within ±1.5 mm (`depth`), every hole a closed chain — not a cut parallel to it, which follows the beads and warp.
- **Cast with machined features:** parting plane first; cast form drafted from it with its large fillets at the casting's T; machined faces as cuts at the machined T, snapped. **Threads go on last:** each is the slowest step of the part (one clamp: 25 s of a 54 s build) and every edit above them plays them again. Rough skin is no finding below its T; a missing hole is. **Pockets** (from one flange housing, 94.4 % at ±0.2, so one part's evidence): one sketch of the pocket outlines, extruded `new` as a tool with their draft, its floor edges rounded, then cut from the block. A fillet naming the tool feature as `body`, with edges by query, goes on each solid it hits. A round at the foot of a revolved boss goes in the revolve's profile, and `reverse_plan_replace` credits it with the revolve as `by`. **Fit a drafted wall where the rounds are not:** `fit: { level, draftDeg }` fits mid-wall and moves each chain to where the taper puts it on the sketch's plane; extrude from there at the same `draftDeg`, toward the level's side. Without it every wall sits (level − plane)·tan(draft) off, 0.13 mm here. Fit pockets without the chain round them: nested inside it they move as a hole does. `sketch.inset` moves several closed chains together.
- **Bent tube and bellows** (one part so far, an exhaust pipe, 87.6 % at ±0.4). Read the centreline with `reverse_scan_tube_trace { start, dir, both: true }`, started on the longest straight: a bend with no straight read beyond it leaves its turn and radius open. Each `stops` entry says where and why; start again past a bellows or flange. Give its `route` to a sweep (`{ kind: "sweep", profile, route }`), so bends in different planes are one feature. Make the profile's plane with `element_construct` from `startPlane`, and take its size from `runs` (major, minor) and its direction from `stations` (majorDir). On this part the trace put the centres nearer than a route read by hand (rms 0.12 against 0.22) and its radii came within 0.5 of a mid-bend section. Check a radius that matters by a section through mid-bend, centroid against the model. Read that section in the bend's plane and across it: a difference means the bends squash the tube, and a profile of sketch shape `{ kind: "ellipse", center, a, b, angleDeg }` gained 4 %. A cylinder fit on a short straight grows into its bends and warns "two surfaces", and a torus fit spreads into the straights or is refused: use the trace for those runs. Bellows: axis through three or more crest-ring fits, pitch from `reverse_section_fit { periodic }` on two planes through the axis, averaged, since a flexed bellows reads differently per side (3.9 % there). On an oval tube keep an opening's cut no deeper than the flange, or it breaks through the wall with no warning.
- **Shelled housings** of analytic faces (extrusions, drafts, revolves): outer form and its rounds, `shell` at the wall (snap it, report the scatter), ribs and towers, openings, remaining rounds. Lofted or fitted skins are the freeform thin shell below.
- **Freeform thin shell** (covers, lamp housings, fairings, bezels). Two took 35 min and a three-copy hack to make hollow; the third shelled at 3.4 mm in 29 s from analytic pieces. Choose the route to the thickness before building.
  - **A decomposition that shells** (the scooter cover, 81 % of the outer skin within ±0.4): the plan as a drafted prism, ∩ (a torus ∪ a fitted B-spline slab), ∩ a visor extrusion, − a rim wedge. The shroud tube is a circle revolved about a far axis, carrying bow and tilt in one; the wing top a `surface` thickened 45 mm down and mirrored; the concave shoulder round goes on the torus ∪ slab union **before** the intersection with the plan prism — on the final body its chain ran on into the plan rounds and failed.
  - **Form from the outer skin only:** `skin: "outer"` (and `within` to leave the rim out) on every section fit, stack and sketch `fit`; the inner skin carries ribs and bosses — a form fitted on it came out R321 for R587. Compare with `skin: "outer"` until the model is hollow.
  - **Thickness, one of three:** (a) **one skin, trimmed, then thickened.** Fit the outer skin oversize: a Patch network's sheet, a `surface` (region by query, `fit.robust`, `expandPct` past every edge) or a sheet. Trim the sheet to the part: `boolean { op: "intersect", body: <sheet>, tool: <outline prism> }` keeps what lies inside the prism (`cut`, what lies outside) — the prism stays a body, for the next sheet — and `split { body: <sheet>, tools: [<sheets or solids>], keep: "scan" }` drops what has no scan behind it (a lip's overshoot past its tip, a sheet's run past another sheet). Then `thicken { sheet, thickness, side }`. It names a face that bends tighter than the wall, and answers the wall reached along the sheet's edge. Trimmed first, the wall's ends stand square to it and round further than a slab cut at a slant. Cut after the thicken (the prism through the slab), the ends run along the cut: take that where the scan's edge runs along the pull. (b) Outer and inner skins both fitted, then `split { body: <block>, tools: [outer, inner], keep: "between" }` — the wall follows the part, no offset. Skins that meet inside the body are refused in a second, saying where: model one skin and thicken it. (c) Inner lofts through inset stations — stations in one call: fit station 1 ("Wing 1") with `fit: { skin: "outer", within, accept: { sigmas: 2, kinds: ["tangent"] } }`, close it with a line across the wall ends at least 2 × the wall below the base (`reverse_sketch_edit`), then one `loft { name: "Wing", sections: [{ sketch: "Wing 1" }, <planes>…], fit: { like: "Wing 1", skin: "outer", within }, inner: 2.4, ruled: false }`. The wall by a parameter: `exprs: { inner: "wall" }` on the loft moves every inner station, `exprs: { inset: "wall" }` on an inset sketch that one. Read each of `stations` (`fit` residuals, `reversed`); cut "Wing inner" from the outer after the joins and the mirror; edit stations later with `reverse_feature_update { features }`. One inner twin alone: a sketch with `inset: { by: 2.4, of: "Wing 1" }`. `like` maps pieces at the same [u, v]: parallel planes, or radial ones — not tilted planes that are not parallel.
  - **A skin that turns** (a flange into a curled lip, a cover into its skirt) is two sheets, each fitted along its own direction:
    1. Split each by the other, `keep: "scan"`.
    2. Join them: `boolean { op: "join", body: A, tool: B }` sews two sheets into one.
    3. Intersect the joined sheet with the outline, so both crease edges end together.
    4. Thicken once, and round the crease on the solid.

    It gives one wall with no notch. Never join two thickened slabs: the last lamp's met with a notch. Never fit one sheet along a compromise direction for both: its U came out 1–1.9 mm shallow. A trimmed sheet left in pieces apart warns that Thicken takes one piece.
  - **Never `shell` a lofted skin.** If an offset is wanted, test `offsetBody` on the raw outer body in one call before blends and trims go on; a failure names the faces and how far each offsets alone.
  - Build every component at least 2 × the wall past the base and trim planes, and trim last.
  - Blends from sections at 3–5 stations (`reverse_body_fillet_read`, or a section fit's arc): a constant radius is a compromise — say so.
  - **The interior** (always, §1): copy the outer form before the shell (`moveBody` copy) and inset it about half a wall (`offsetBody`), so ribs end inside the wall instead of splitting the outer skin; clip each rib, boss and skirt with it by `boolean { op: "intersect", keep: true }` — one form clips them all — then join each to the part. Ribs and bosses up to the inner skin: `extent: { kind: "toNext" }` with `join`. The opening is cut last, ledges and seats after it.
  - **Openings:** after a cylinder fit, slice the opening front, rear and sides. A vertical wall on one side and a tilted one on another is two surfaces — the cylinder ∩ a wall limiter, not one compromise cylinder (the last cover's speedo hole fitted one tilted cylinder at σ 0.12 that cut its own front wall).
  - **Handed parts:** check every mirrored detail on both halves before mirroring it — the last cover's two handlebar openings differed (RMS 1.95 mirrored, 0.39 fitted on its own side).
- **Rotationally patterned part** (fan, impeller, wheel). Datum: the bore's axis, square to the face it mates on (§2 datum check — not a pad floor). Counts: `scan_pattern`, one radius band per feature (blades, holes, pads, bosses). Hub, dish and flange: one profile on a radial section between features (`section_cut { across: { axis, angleDeg } }` at the answer's `betweenDeg`, or a sketch on `{ kind: "radial", axis, angleDeg }`), revolved once — after reading the drift per height (`reverse_section_stack { axis }`, `fromAxis`) to choose the angle. One blade, **tall, round, then cut** — for any blade, rib or fin that fades into a surface. Before the plan is set, read its crest along its length (`scan_heightmap`, or radial cuts at the blade's angles): the line it runs on and how it blends into the hub top; a few cross-sections read it 0.1–0.2 mm low on the last fan. Its plan fitted on one section, then **one extrude** with `draftDeg` from a sketch above the blade's top, down past the dish — the plan inset by the draft to that height (`inset`), so each flank is one drafted face, as on the part. A taper through the sketch plane is two faces meeting at that plane (`draftBothWays` says so): on the last fan one flank's roots would not round until it was one extrude, though on a plain test blade two ways rounded more often than one, so which builds is the shape's. A flank's roots that fail: try the blade the other way before a smaller radius. A revolved envelope (`boolean` intersect) trims its foot inside the dish wall and leaves the top standing above the crest. **Check the one blade before the pattern:** `reverse_body_compare { feature: <the blade> }` — both flanks within T along the whole length, inner end to tip (its top is meant to read high until the cut); a plan fitted at one height carries its error into every copy (an earlier fan's 14 blades all read red on one flank and blue on the other from one fitted plan). Then `pattern { mode: "circular", count, step: 360 }` and join. Root rounds next, on the tall blades: **a round fails wherever the blade's height goes to zero**, at any radius and at a varying one, so the root must never meet that place. **A root chain must end on sharp corners**, so keep the dish's rim round and the hub's edge rounds out of the profile and add them as fillets after the roots — a chain that runs into a round it meets fails whole. Read one root first (`reverse_body_fillet_read`), then one fillet with `edges: [{ sides: { madeBy: ["Blade", "Dish"] } }]` on the joined body — never `madeBy` of the pattern. Then **one revolved cut along the measured crest**, tangent into the hub top so it leaves no seam there: it gives every blade its top and runs through the root rounds, which so follow each blade up into the hub. Then the top rounds and the rim's. **Never move a surface off the scan to make a round build:** a cut on the measured crest is the part; a blade shortened to r 83 to dodge a rim, or a top left 0.5 mm off, is not. A feature that failed and that this construction made needless goes in with `reverse_plan_replace`. A boolean naming the pattern takes the original and every copy (`took` counts them).
- **Volute / spiral casing** (a pump's or a blower's housing). A radial station every 20–30° (`{ kind: "radial", axis, angleDeg }`); station 1 fitted, the others in the loft's own call: `loft { sections: [...], fit: { like: <station 1> } }`. Where the scan has no clean section (the cutwater), draw that station. Check each station's pieces before anything takes it — `fitted[].like`, `crosses`, a piece refused as too short, reversed or under 5° — then inset. The exterior is the stations inset by the wall (`inset: { by, of }`), lofted `ruled: true`. The tangential outlet is lofted on parallel planes, then joined. A station turned later takes `set: { angleDeg }`, its features following.

- **Smooth solid casting** (die-cast or forged, solid rather than shelled: a mirror foot, a handle, a lever, a bracket with sculpted form — blended everywhere, large varying blends, no flat facets beyond the mating faces). **The form comes from sections, never from a plan prism cut by planes.**
  - **Walls:** each `curvedRuns` entry with an `along` direction is a profile extruded that way: a section square to `along`, sketch `fit` on it (`skin: "outer"`), extrude along it. A wall whose section changes along the part: a loft through fitted stations (model §2b). A band that runs round the plan with its own profile (a styling swage, a bead): its section fitted, then `sweep` along the plan's chain drawn as its own sketch.
  - **Large blends are form:** read them at stations first (`reverse_body_fillet_read`, or the section fit's arcs). Over about 2 mm they go into the profiles and stations as tangent arcs; only small rounds are end fillets. A blend that varies along an edge: the read's `[rStart, rEnd]` as a variable fillet when its residual is within T; otherwise into the stations.
  - **Bounding:** a wall that is the surface only over part of the part is cut `toFace` or `toBody` from a sketch of its region — never an infinite plane or a helper block, whose faces stay on the part as seams.
  - **Then** the analytic features: bores, bosses, seats, pockets, holes, at their nominals.
  - Target 85 % within T. Each loop's review on such a part is about form: a band of red or blue along a blend is the blend's radius, a band along a wall is the wall's profile.
- **Faceted housing** (a gem-cut consumer product: about 25 planes and their rounds) — **only where `scan_segment` finds no `curvedRuns` there and a section across each pair of neighbours shows a crease**; strips at σ near `maxRms` are a smooth solid (above). Find the facets first (`scan_segment`). Build a half block from the datum to the back. Add one `cutPlane` per fitted facet, every plane that crosses the mirror plane symmetrised (`plane-coords`, the off-axis component 0). Then `mirror`. Add-on bands (a button panel) are bodies of their own, trimmed by every neighbouring facet plane, then joined — an infinite plane otherwise leaves a ledge. A band that reads convex (an arc across it, sag over T) is an arc-profile sweep, not a plane.
- **Moulded housing with keys** (the last housing, 96 % within T): bands split at the measured parting lines. Twisted faces as ruled lofts through bilinear fits (`scan_fit_surface`). Each key a loft intersected with a side-view outline prism, so its ends are steps, not ramps. The housing beside the keys is flat facets (seed-grown). Parting grooves are a chamfer on one band's edge. A core block fills the inside (below), then `mirror`. The asymmetric details come after the mirror: a button from a height map in its face's frame, covers from fitted planes and their rim groove, slots and channels, engraved icons with their floors offset. Edge rounds last.
- **Banded constructions leave voids.** Bands, lofts and station profiles that stop short of the mirror plane can close a cavity inside the solid; the last housing carried 1.2 cm³ of it through three STEPs. Add a core block, or run every profile to the symmetry plane; `reverse_body_check`'s `voids` is the gate.
- **Freeform, the surface route** (only what the user agreed to): **a Patch network first, Fit Surface for a single gentle patch.** Either gives a fitted surface model — a skin on the scan's evidence, not a designer's parametric model: say so in the reply and the report.
  - **Which one.** A Patch network (`reverse_network_create`) where the skin folds back (it wraps past half a cylinder — a trigger's end, a grip, a shell round a shoulder: no height field holds it), where several patches meet and must run on tangent, or where creases and parting lines cross it — its patches share their curves exactly, meet tangent across every curve not a crease, and are fitted together. A `surface` where one gentle patch, a height field over its own plane, covers the region up to its crease — a cover panel, a hump's side. Several surfaces meet only where a `trim` cuts them, as creases, never tangent.
  - **Laying a network out.** Curves where the part's surfaces would meet: along every crease and parting line — `reverse_network_suggest_creases { box? }` traces the sharp ridges and folds, each candidate's `curve` ready to pass on as it is (`crease: true`) — and across the skin in a grid of four-sided cells that follows the curvature (the curvature colouring shows it; fewer, larger cells where the skin is gentle). Three-, five- and six-sided cells are split from their centre; two or more than six are refused. Each curve is its points on the scan in order — end, grips, end (`scan_nearest` for them); a point within `snap` (0.5 mm) of a node, a grip or a curve given before it joins it there, so write a grid as its lines one way, then the lines the other way through their points. Curves crossing between their points make no node. `fill: "all"` fills every closed region. Read `curves` (the ids each became), `joined`, `openEnds` — a curve end nothing meets closes nothing — and `refused`.
  - **Judging it.** `reverse_network_read`: each patch's `rms` and `max` against T and σ_scan, `worstAngle` (under 0.5° across tangent curves), `worstGap` (microns), each crease's `fold`, the corners where three, five or six patches meet with their angle. A patch over T: more spans across it (`reverse_network_edit { set: [{ curve, spans }] }`) or a curve through it (`add`); a curve that should be a fold: `set: [{ curve, crease: true }]`; a point in the wrong place: `move`. Each edit is one undo step.
  - **The body** is a sheet, or a solid where the network closes. A sheet goes into `trim`, `thicken`, `split` and `boolean` as a fitted surface does, and into the STEP as a skin. A network need not end on the part's edge: lay it past the edge and trim the sheet back with the outline (`boolean { op: "intersect" }`, §4a thin shell), rather than run a cell's side along a rim; compare it with `reverse_body_compare { bodies: [name] }`.
  - **A `surface`**: its `region` a query — `{ box, normal, exclude }` or `{ marked: true }`, never a vertex list — up to its crease, no fold-back; `fit.robust: true` where a rib or boss lies in it, read `result.surface` (rms, worst points). A refused or robust fit names its `strays` — a second sheet under the region, far points — each with a `box` ready for `region.exclude`: give them and fit again. **Past its region** the margin is extrapolated: `result.surface.pastRegion { off, at }` says how far it stands off the scan there (a warning past 0.2) — take less `expandPct` or a larger region. Split by it, the answer's `offScan` is how far the cut face lies off the scan outside the region; a band or swage across a wall the rest of the part keeps is `split { within: mm }` — the sheet cuts only inside its region's outline grown by that margin. A region read by `normal` is within its angle by construction: don't widen `withinDeg` for a spread line; a net over 20 × 20 is refused. `shape: "plane"` or `"cylinder"` where it is one. Read each σ against T. One `trim` closes them; "not closed" counts the open edges — where the next patch goes.
  - Analytic features afterwards. **A shell that wraps round a shoulder** with constant sections along it may still be radial stations and a loft (model §2b) — try the section stack first (§1, freeform triage).

## 5. The design loop and validation

**The loop, after every feature group from the first on:**
1. `reverse_body_review { tolerance: T }` — the volume error and the live compare in one call: `places` ranked by volume and depth (each missing, excess or off, with mm³, depth, where, the nearest face's maker and the plan's region), a picture of the map on the scan with the places numbered, the `trend` against the last and the first review, the target's verdict, and `next`.
   Reading its fields: a place's `at` and `box` are what `view_render { frame }` and `section_render { frame: { world } }` take; `mean` is signed as compare reads it, positive where the model lies inside the scan; `by` says what saw the place (volume, map, both, or far — past the reach); `score.withinT` counts every vertex read or far, so material not modelled counts against it; every review is kept in the session for `trend`; `target` is the plan's verdict with its `next` when not done.
2. **Look at the picture.** A review past the answer limit answers queued, and `scanruler_wait { for: "job" }` brings the picture with its answer. It is inline on purpose: the colours say where and what kind (a band along an edge is a blend, a field across a wall is the wall's form, a spot is a feature missing); the numbers say how much.
3. **Work on place 1** — read the profile across it first (below), then fix the design there: the profile, the station, the radius, the missing feature. Not the place's symptom: a red band along every blend is one decision about blends, not one fillet.
4. Review again. A place that does not move after two tries: say so in the chat with its numbers and what you tried, and ask — never drop it into the report silently.

The run ends when the plan's verdict says met (§0: the target, the regions, nothing removed unbuilt, no feature patch over T), or when the person signs off on less. A feature removed after failing is not a step forward: the review's `removed` names it until a feature of that name builds, or `reverse_plan_replace` names the built feature that made it needless, and the verdict stays "not done" while it does. A review is a few kB; the full answers (`reverse_body_compare` without `detail`, `reverse_body_check { audit: "full" }`) are for one feature's question or the end.

**Numbers classify, renders locate.** Judge deviations from the answers at T; a render shows where and the model's topology, never "how bad" from colours. **Before a fix, read the profile**: scan against model along a line across the place (`section_render { section }` and its `gap`, or `scan_heightmap { plane, of: "deviation" }`). On the last housing, guesses from colours went wrong three times: a groove inside instead of outside, a plateau clip, a lug's shape.

- **A picture with `show: { map: true }`** waits for the map; `mapWhy` "not read after N s" says how far it got: ask again in a while, what the worker read is kept.
- **A picture with no colours** says why: in the Deviation workspace `view_render` answers `map` (which map it painted) or `map: null` with `mapWhy` — the switch off (`deviation_settings { show: true }`), no map measured, the reference side not measured. `mapNote` says when the reference drawn over the scan dulls it: `show: { reference: false }`. In a batch every picture goes to a file (its `path`, or a numbered one in `scanruler-work` inside the scan's folder) and the result names it.
- **A section that grazes a drafted wall** reads red all along it: cut square to the feature, or judge it with `reverse_body_compare { feature }`.
- **Colour maps vary:** read `settings.colormap` (`deviation_settings {}`) before reading colours, or set `colormap: "jet"` and tell the user. On viridis dark is *inside*, not *bad*. Count deep-inside scan from `farInside`, not from violet.
- **One feature, one call:** `reverse_body_compare { feature, range: T, picture: true }` — the scan read about that feature's faces alone, its patches, the far reading near it, `numbers` (its radius, distance, draft or end beside the scan's reading now, in sigmas) and a picture of a section across it. Check each feature so after building it, in place of a compare, a describe and a render. A feature in several places: `picture.places` lists them, `at` picks the one cut. On a patterned or many-faced feature keep it small: `patches: 5, spots: 0, faces: 3`.
- **Volume during the round, not only at the end** — the review runs it; alone: `reverse_body_volume_compare { tolerance: T }` after each major step, and again before a round ends — before the reply, the report, or a note's resolve. A `lump` over a few mm³, or any region deeper than 2T, is worked on in that round: fix the design, compare again. Only what cannot be fixed goes in the reply, with its volume and the reason. A round never ends on a volume reading taken before its last change; the report's validation carries the last one. The last clamp's nose and ear (9.7 mm³) were found only because a round began with a volume compare — the two before had ended without one — and the last housing's was run once, after its first pass.
- **Volume first** after each major step: `reverse_body_volume_compare { tolerance: T }` — the bodies and the scan as solids, about 15 s. `missing` is material the part has and the model lacks (a boss, rib or flange not modelled), `excess` material the model has and the part has not (a hole or pocket not cut, a wall too thick); each region with its volume, depth, `shape` and the body `face` and `madeBy` nearest its deepest point. **A `lump` of more than a few mm³ is a feature missing or extra: model it or name it before reading patches.** A `layer` is a face standing off — the patches say by how much. `unsure` is where the scan does not say: never call it an error. It sees nothing thinner than `smallestSeen`; look with `view_render { frame: region.box }`.
- **Every compare carries `volume`** — the last volume compare's totals, or "volume not compared since step N": that line means run `reverse_body_volume_compare` before the next step.
- **Live compare** — the review runs it at `detail: "score"`; alone: `reverse_body_compare { bodies, range: T }` — its RMS counts scan within 10 × range of the model; the Deviation workspace's counts all within `maxDistance` and reads higher: quote which. **`far` first**: `far.inside` is material the part has not (a hole, pocket or slot not cut, a blind bore); `far.outside` is material the model lacks, or spray. Then `patches` — one face each, with box, extreme and the feature that made it; look with `view_render { frame: patch.box }` and `scan_query { box }`. Every review miss so far was a patch or a far cluster. Pass bodies explicitly; compare halves with `within`. A patch's `face` (index) and `madeBy` are describe's. Each scan point is read against a surface facing its way (`facingDeg`, 90°), so the far skin of a thin wall no longer reads as −t; `side` counts what was re-faced, dropped (`unfacing: "drop"`) or, with `skin: "outer"`, left out as inner skin. A sheet is read on its front only.
- **On a big scan, validate locally:** after a step, `section_render { plane, offsets }` across the worst region (from the last compare's patches) is cheaper and says more than another whole-body compare.
- **Look at the model:** model-only close-ups at every feature and every transition, silhouettes of scan and model alone from one side. `view_render { show: { map: true, bodies: false } }` puts the map on the scan alone.
- **Both maps.** The map on the scan finds what the model lacks; it cannot show model material the part has not (a stud through a wall, a wall too long) or model surface nothing was scanned at. `reverse_body_compare_in_deviation` answers both: read `onReference` — `far.outside` and `patches` with side `outside` are model material the part has not, each with its face and `madeBy`; `noScan` (and `noScanPct`) is model surface on no evidence: every such patch is inferred (P/I in the report) or wrong. It takes a sheet when named, and turns the facing limit on for it (`facingOn`). `deviation_settings { on: "reference" }` paints it on the model for a picture; `on: "scan"` back.
- **Final, in the Deviation workspace:** `reverse_body_compare_in_deviation { tolerance: T, range: 3T, maxDistance }` (**no best fit** — it would hide the offset; it sets the three as `deviation_settings` does and answers them in `reading`); `deviation_hotspots` and its `far`, then `deviation_hotspots { on: "reference" }` and its `far` and `noScan`; iso, bottom, top and side at `width: 1400` with `path`, `inline: false` and `show: { notes: false }` (no `sketches` key there), then `on: "reference"` and the model side's iso and most-inferred side the same way, colour map and range named — those are the saved copies; **look at both maps inline** at the default width before the handover (`view_render { views }` on each side), since the model-only renders hide what the maps show; read the answer's `plan` — the target met or not, regions open, features removed — and say it in the reply; `reverse_export_step { path, verify: true }` last — `verify.ok`, or fix each of `verify.problems` (a loop the wrong way round is a round Onshape and SolidWorks draw as a cove; `loops.unread` counts faces whose loops are not read, B-spline ones — not a fault); `reverse_body_check` valid, closed, one solid.
- **Every patch and far cluster** is (a) an error to fix, (b) a documented omission or artefact, (c) texture — `kind: "texture"`, shallow, small, on both sides of the model and spread over the skin — counted once with its share (`features.shareOfExcess` is the rest), (d) a physical asymmetry or warp — reported, not modelled. A `kind: "feature"` patch is never (c): it is material missing or misplaced (a pad, a rib, a step, a wrong height) — model it, or dismiss it only with a section across it showing the model follows the scan. A broad, shallow patch on one side (100 mm² at 0.4 mm) can read texture and still be a face at the wrong height: section it. On the last pump every person's note sat on a feature patch the run had called scatter. On a rough casting the RMS is the casting: judge the model by `far` and the machined faces at their T. Say what enforcing symmetry cost.
- **Critical features one by one** against the inventory, nominal beside measured.

### Before every handover
The model guide's §3, and:
- `reverse_export_step { verify: true }` may answer queued on a big part: `scanruler_wait` it. An answer that says `truncated` is read with `scanruler_read { path: file }`, in parts, when you cannot open the folder.
- After the last change or note round, run the final compare and the volume compare again and rewrite every number and picture in the report from them. Nothing "will be redone"; the last pump's report went out with the pictures and RMS of two rounds before.
- Both maps read (§5): nothing on the model side standing proud that is not intent; every **no scan** area named as inferred.
- Every hotspot over T on the scan side is modelled or named in the report — a row of small ones at a pitch is a feature (tabs, clips, ribs), not scatter.
- The plan: every region ticked or skipped with its reason; the target met, or "not done" in the reply with what is left; every feature removed after failing named.

## 6. Threads
Signs: a striped wall, an irregular slice, a cylinder fit with high sigma. `reverse_thread_read` along each small bore (a fitted cylinder, the modelled face, or a line), batched: pitch, minor, major, hand, `suggested`, and along a face the `feature` ready to add. `thread: false` beside a pitch is a ripple within the scan's noise (`contrast` against `noise`) — a plain bore. Size from the crest Ø (≈ minor, slightly large): crest 5.1–5.2 rules out M5 → M6×1. If the user names a size the evidence contradicts, show the evidence and make the change trivial if they insist.

## 7. Report (`<part>_REPORT.md`)
Only numbers you computed. 1 Files (with colour map and range). 2 Scan: size, rims (missing vs physical), artefacts, not modelled, asymmetry, σ_scan. 3 Class and T, analytic vs not, freeform outcome. 4 Datums and residuals, STEP re-read. 5 Modelling approach. 6 Parameters: name | value | measured basis | nominal and why. 7 Features: feature | function | measured | nominal | **M** measured / **P** partly supported / **I** inferred. 8 Assumptions and limitations. 9 Validation: build status, validity, volume; coverage, RMS, mean, % within ±T, p95 (machined faces apart); `far` both sides; the model side: % backed by scan, % within ±T, its `far.outside` and the **no scan** areas with what was inferred there; every remaining patch on either map with its cause (a–d); the loops — how many reviews, and the trend from the first to the last. 10 Revision log.

## 8. Final reply
The target first — met, or **not done** with the open places; then class, T and the headline numbers; the judgement calls; remaining deviations and what could not be done; notes resolved and still open; the files. No recap of every step.

## Fallbacks

Only when no call above answers the question — and say in the report what was done outside ScanRuler. Each line names what replaced the old route first.

- **Fitting lines, arcs and tangent blends in numpy/scipy** → the profile road (§3): `reverse_section_fit`, a sketch with `fit`, relations from `reverse_sketch_candidates`, the solver's `solve.fit`. A freeform curve no run of arcs holds (a cam, a grip, a blend) is a spline piece: a sketch with `fit: { chains: [i], spline: n }` (more points where it bends more; read `solve.fit`), or a `spline` entity with its `pts`, held `tangent` where an end is coincident with a line's or an arc's. Python only for a curve ScanRuler has no piece for (an ellipse).
- **Exporting the scan (`export_cloud`, the PLY route) to slice and fit it elsewhere** → `reverse_section_stack` and `reverse_section_fit`, batched; a sloped, twisted or quadric patch → `scan_fit_surface`. Export only for an analysis no command offers (a free-form surface's own coefficients), and slice it in the frame ScanRuler shows.
- **Exporting the model as STL to measure scan-to-model distances** → `reverse_body_compare` with `far`, `within` and `patches`; the whole field as data, per vertex → `deviation_export`.
- **Region-growing planes or drawing local maps on an exported cloud** → `scan_segment`, `element_fit { at }` and `scan_heightmap { plane, of }`.
- **A KD-tree search for the mirror plane** → `element_construct` plane-symmetry.
- **Plotting a slice with its fit (matplotlib)** → `section_render`; the numbers come from its `gap` and from `reverse_section_fit`, not from the plot.

## Red flags

| Symptom | What it means | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| A round's `warning` "radius not read" | The corner had under three slice points — its pieces were taken out — or hit the search's limit | Give `radius`, or fit the corner again with its pieces |
| A form fit or compare reads the wall as −t, ribs in the profile | Both skins read | `skin: "outer"` on the fit, stack or compare |
| `element_fit` refused "(axis off by N°: axis … through …)" | The surface found leans off the axis given | Give that `axis`, or drop it |
| `far.inside` high | Material the part has not: a hole, pocket or slot not cut | Look at each far patch's box; cut it |
| `far.outside` high | A flange, boss or rib not modelled, or spray | Model it, or show it is spray |
| `onReference.far.outside` or `outside` patches | Model material the part has not: a stud through a wall, a wall or lip too long | Trim it; its `face.madeBy` is the feature |
| `noScanPct` high, a `noScan` patch | Model surface no scan stands behind | Inferred: say so (P/I) — or it is not on the part |
| A sketch with more than ten short lines in a row | A polyline where the part has a curve | Refit the chain as lines, arcs or a spline (`fit` with `within`) |
| A fit's `fitted` entry says "profile crosses itself" | The fitted pieces cross: one outline gives several regions | Refit with `within`, a higher `detail` or less `closeGaps`; `accept` is refused until it is one region |
